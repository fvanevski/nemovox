# Architecture Assessment: Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe NVFP4 vs WhisperX / Pyannote

## Answers to the 10 Architectural Questions

1. **Does the NVFP4 checkpoint actually run on the RTX 5090?**
   **Yes.** With vLLM `0.32.0`, NeMo Speech (`99c252a7`), pre-installed `flashinfer-cubin` + `flashinfer-jit-cache` (`sm120f`), `VLLM_DISABLED_KERNELS=FlashInferFP8ScaledMMLinearKernel`, and `--kv-cache-dtype fp8`, the checkpoint loads cleanly into `19.28 GiB` VRAM with `quantization=modelopt_mixed` (zero BF16 fallback) and sustains up to 23-minute single-request audio within `28.8 GiB` peak VRAM (`--gpu-memory-utilization 0.78`, `32,768` max context).

2. **Can it produce usable source-language transcripts in French and Mandarin?**
   **Yes, for utterances >=5–10s in pure source language.** It achieved `10.20%` WER on French FLEURS, `10.67%` CER (explicit) / `12.36%` CER (auto) on Mandarin FLEURS (`0.0%` CER on 3 of 6 clips), and significantly outperformed WhisperX on several French cartoon dialogue lines (e.g., preserving `"Isabella, Nadia"` and disfluencies). However, it shows Katakana cross-script leakage on foreign loanwords in Mandarin (`Scotturb` -> `スコートブ`) and severe hallucination/language drift on isolated 2–3s clips (`05:39`, `22:08`).

3. **Can it distinguish speakers more reliably than WhisperX/Pyannote?**
   **Yes on 15–120s segments; NO on unchunked 5–23 minute recordings.** On the 39s child-voices clip where Pyannote collapsed two children into one `SPEAKER_04`, Nemotron cleanly separated `<spk:0>` and `<spk:1>`. On the 100s continuous clip, it produced 4 coherent speakers. However, on 300s it collapsed to 2 alternating tags, and on the 23-minute full episode it suffered severe word-level `<spk:N>` ping-ponging inside single sentences.

4. **Can it represent simultaneous speakers with independently timed words?**
   **Partially / Yes in `multispeaker_sot` mode, with CTC limitations.** Both `fr_2spk_overlap` and `zh_2spk_overlap` recovered both simultaneous lexical streams as interleaved `<spk:0>` / `<spk:1>` turns, and the CTC aligner produces separate per-speaker aligned word streams plus Sortformer multi-speaker `diarization` intervals. However, if any word/character is OOV for the CTC head, the entire alignment aborts.

5. **Can its CTC output replace language-specific WhisperX forced alignment?**
   **Not unconditionally yet.** While CTC alignment works out-of-the-box for French (`100%` timed words) and most Mandarin (`5/6` clips) without downloading per-language Wav2Vec2 models, its `23,862`-token CTC vocabulary rejects valid infrequent CJK characters (e.g. `'钾'`) generated by the 131K-vocab LLM backbone, causing **all-or-nothing `alignment_failed`** (`words=[]`) for the entire recording. Any production replacement must either patch/fall back per-utterance on CTC OOV errors or retain a secondary forced-aligner fallback.

6. **Can its diarization output replace Pyannote?**
   **For short-to-medium chunks (<=120s), yes; for full episodes, not without chunked speaker-embedding reconciliation.** Nemotron's bundled Sortformer (`Nemotron-3-Diarization`) runs inside the single perception pass and handled child voices better than Pyannote's clustering on `03:16–03:55`. But single-pass 23-minute decoding destabilizes `<spk:N>` attribution in the LLM text output, and chunked processing does not share speaker IDs across chunks without external speaker embedding matching.

7. **Does it offer a meaningful reduction in the number of models and pipeline stages?**
   **Yes — huge operational simplification in principle.** WhisperX + Wav2Vec2 forced alignment + Pyannote requires 3+ separate model families, CUDA 12/13 `libcublas` shims, and `HF_TOKEN` gated Pyannote downloads. Nemotron unifies ASR + diarization + word/character alignment into a single vLLM service running `50–78x` real-time.

8. **Can the new project expose a stable multilingual transcript contract?**
   **Yes.** `nemotron-transcript-contract/v1` cleanly decouples source transcription, speaker turns, offset-adjusted CTC words/characters, Sortformer diarization, and optional translation, and adapts to `/workspace/pyannote`'s SRT/ASS subtitle writer (noting that CJK line-wrapping requires non-whitespace splitting in future subtitle rendering).

9. **Is direct English translation useful, or should translation remain a separate stage?**
   **Keep translation as a separate stage** (at least for high-accuracy archival subtitling). Nemotron's direct speech-to-English translation is fast (`0.2–1.7s`) and generally readable, but dropped a key noun (`"钙"` / calcium) in Mandarin and misrendered names/idioms in French (`"mu"` -> `"dead language"`, `"Nadia"` -> `"the giraffe"`). Translating from a verified source transcript preserves traceability and allows context/glossary injection.

10. **Is its GPU memory and runtime cost acceptable for local media-archive processing?**
    **Yes, if the host GPU is dedicated during batch runs.** At `~28.8 GiB` peak VRAM (`19.28 GiB` weights) on a 32 GB RTX 5090, it cannot stay resident alongside a 28 GB local LLM (`ninfer-serve`), just like WhisperX requires unloading the LLM — **but** at `50–78x` real-time (`17.8s` for a 23-minute episode!), a full episode finishes in under 20 seconds.

## Recommendation
**Do not immediately replace `/workspace/pyannote` wholesale with single-pass full-episode Nemotron.** Instead, **adopt a staged hybrid / chunked architecture** if migrating:
1. Run Nemotron NVFP4 over 60–120s overlapping audio chunks (where its ASR, overlap handling, and Sortformer diarization are strongest) rather than 23-minute single-pass requests.
2. Add a graceful fallback for CTC `alignment_failed` (especially CJK OOV characters like `'钾'`) and cross-chunk speaker ID linking.
3. Keep English translation as a separate text-to-text pass anchored to `source_text` rather than replacing source transcripts.
